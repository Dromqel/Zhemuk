# Zhemuk

> A river of glass flows through the forest where the dragon planted its teeth.

## The Trees That Sang Without Wind

The forest begins beyond a bridge of ordinary stone. Its trees are dark, its soil pale, and its branches make a thin ringing sound whenever someone on the bridge speaks a name.

A river crosses the forest without wetting its banks. It is made of clear glass that moves slowly downstream. Leaves rest inside it rather than upon it, turning between layers as if carried through the pages of a book.

At the bridge stands a keeper with a little bronze rake. She gathers the shadows left by travelers who pause too long to watch the current. She hangs those shadows on the bridge rail until their owners remember to look behind them.

The oldest shadow has been there for forty years. It resembles a person holding a seed between both hands.

## The Traveler Who Carried a Branch

A traveler arrived carrying a branch wrapped in wool. He had cut it from a tree growing through the floor of an empty house. When he removed the branch, the tree's reflection remained upright in the window, though the tree itself bent toward the door.

The keeper placed his branch against the rail. The oldest shadow reached toward it without leaving its nail.

The traveler remembered the house's former occupant, a woman who had taught him to plant seeds in broken cups. She had said that a root needed to know where the container ended before deciding which way to grow.

He had returned years later to find the cups empty and the tree rising through the floor. In its bark was a narrow opening shaped like the handle of the cup he had once broken himself.

## A Crossing Below the Surface

The keeper raked the glass river beside the bridge. Its surface parted without cracking, showing a path lined with little pale objects. The traveler thought they were stones until he noticed the roots growing from them.

They were teeth. Each held a tree inside its clear enamel. At the path's end stood a cup made of dark wood, large enough for someone to sit within it.

The oldest shadow slipped from the rail. It entered the path and moved toward the cup, carrying its invisible seed. The traveler followed with the wrapped branch, while the keeper stayed above and held the river apart with her bronze rake.

Around him, trapped leaves moved through the glass. Each showed the empty house at a different hour, its window full of a tree that had never learned to stand outside.

## The Forest's Buried Mouth

The teeth belonged to a dragon whose jaw lay beneath both banks. Its roots had become the forest. Its breath, cooling between the trees, had formed the moving glass river.

The woman from the house had once planted one of its teeth, believing it a seed polished smooth by water. The tree that grew from it carried the memory of the creature's mouth into her rooms. Its reflection had been waiting for a road back to the forest.

The traveler looked toward the cup at the path's end. Inside it sat the woman, older than he remembered, holding a little root between finger and thumb.

She did not seem surprised to see him. She pointed toward his wrapped branch, then toward the shadow beside him. Between the two, a tiny clear leaf began to form.

## The Four Rings Beneath the Bark

The branch opened along its grain, revealing four marks:

*Zhe — muk — il — or.*

The traveler read them toward the wooden cup. The clear leaf widened until it showed the broken cup from his childhood. Its missing handle rested beneath the soil like a small pale arch.

He placed the branch through that arch. The old shadow knelt beside it and opened its hands. What it had carried was no seed, but the shape of the missing handle, dark and warm.

The two shapes fitted together. In the glass around them, the house's tree straightened. Its window reflection stepped backward into the forest, leaving an ordinary empty room behind.

The woman set down her little root. She lifted the repaired cup, now no larger than it had been when he was a child, and gave it to him without speaking.

## The Bridge With One Less Shadow

The traveler returned above the glass carrying a cup of ordinary soil. The branch was gone. The oldest shadow no longer hung from the bridge rail, though its nail remained warm until evening.

At the house, the tree had disappeared. Its opening in the floor was filled with pale earth. He placed the cup beside the window, where sunlight touched it without showing anything beyond the room.

In spring, a little shoot appeared. Its leaves were dark green, and when the window was open they moved in the same wind as the trees outside.

The forest still rings when names are spoken on the bridge. The river still carries leaves between its moving layers.

But beneath the banks, one buried tooth has grown a root into the dragon's sleeping jaw, and the keeper sometimes hears a sound that resembles a mouth closing gently around a word it has finally remembered.
